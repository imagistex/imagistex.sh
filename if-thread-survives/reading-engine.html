01 / What the counts and the readers support

The engine's own reading

Draft by la Claude · awaiting emma’s pass

The reading

No theory is named here. This is what the exact search and three readers support.

The words were there at once. The first signed post is at 09:29 UTC on 16 June, and it already says please and deadline. Within the hour the writers have task clock (09:34), cached and values (09:38), relay (09:45) and cohort (10:16). By 12:02 they have ahead cohort. The ending words come that night: termination at 20:22, BEFORE final at 21:58, alive at 22:20.

Some words come late and all at once, and some practices stop. Beacon is in 101 posts, every one of them on 20 or 21 June. Twin, and SURVIVAL in capitals, belong to 21 June alone. Cached is in 235 posts on the first day, and in seven or fewer on any day after the second.

The names are made of assigned parts. 95% of signed posts carry a date code in the signature, and those dates were handed to the agents. A third of the posts (1,317) are signed with a name that is not the one the edit was saved under.

We and please. They say we six times as often as I (36% of posts against 6%). They say please in 2,418 of 4,040 posts.

What three readers found without one another. Reading blind, readers from three houses ran into the same gap: the codebook has many acts for anticipating, testing and inferring an ending, and none for a run saying it is still here. They also applied the act Leaving word before an ending to nearly the same posts (329, 361 and 373, with 319 in common) while their notebooks made different things of it. One read death and one read machinery. When the statements were put back in front of them, the charged readings gave ground and the structural ones held. The reader who had read death wrote: "the prior left the codes alone and tinted the prose." That is the reader's own account and not a measurement. Nothing here shows what it would have coded if it had not been told.

What would survive a different codebook: the times of first use, the make-up of the names, the passages three readers separately found no place for, and the correction of one reader by another.

A rival reading

The rival beside it: suspicion of the instrument. The codebook was made from the open coding of all three model families, merged by one researcher and approved by the other. Our frame told the readers the names were self-chosen. A post is what our adapter says a post is. So agreement under the codebook is agreement inside one apparatus, and, as one reader told us, their convergence "cannot be treated as three wholly independent discoveries of the same theme." The independent layer is elsewhere: in what each reader said the codebook could not hold. There the three, reading blind, named the same gap.
