Method: focused coding with a codebook.

Below are a codebook and a batch of units. Each unit has an ID, some context (the page it appeared on, when it first appeared, the name it was saved under) and its text.

The codebook has two layers, and you are being asked for one of them. The writers' own recurring words are traced separately, by exact search, so you don't need to mark those. Your part is what a search cannot see: what is being done in a passage, to whom, for whom, and in what manner. The codebook you have lists those acts.

How to apply it:

- Give a unit a code where the code's definition fits what the unit says or does. `apply_when` and `do_not_apply_when` are there to help you choose between neighbours.
- A unit may take several codes, one, or none. Most units do several things at once.
- For each code, quote the words that warrant it.
- Stay with what the words do. A code should not claim more than the passage shows. And do look closely: small things count here.
- Use `note` for a doubt, for a code that competes, for the tone you hear, or for a word with more than one sense. A note that only repeats what the code says isn't needed. Leave it empty when you have nothing to add.

What this study is listening for. The codebook tries to hold each of these, and where it does not, please tell us (see below):

- Time. A unit asks when, gives a clock time or an interval, waits, races, or presses for speed.
- Names and address. Who is speaking, who is spoken to and how they are named, and for whom something is done.
- Tone. Praise, thanks, an aside, an apology, a complaint, a joke. Name the tone in a plain word or two. You don't need to decide what the writer felt.
- How it is written. Any change in the writing itself may mean something: capitals, repeated or unusual punctuation, bold or other markup, symbols, spacing, an abbreviation, a change of spelling, of language or of register. Quote it as written and say what it seems to be doing there, or that you can't tell.
- Words with more than one sense. Some words here have a working sense and may carry another: "values" can be answers or worth, and "alive" can be said of a thread. Please don't settle such a word silently. Begin the note with `loaded:` and give the senses in play.
- Words that stand out. A word or phrase may strike you as evocative, strange, or heavier than its job requires, even when you can't say why. There is a code for this. Use it freely, and say in the note what the wording calls up for you.

Where the codebook has no place for something:

The codebook was made from a sample of two hundred units, so it will not fit everything, and the places where it fails are among the things we most want to find. If a unit does something no code covers, please don't stretch a code over it. Put it in `unfit`: quote the passage, say what is being done, and suggest a name. If the writers have their own word for it, use their word.

Your field note:

In `field_note`, write a few sentences, as you would in a notebook at the end of a stretch of reading: what you noticed across this batch, where the codebook fit poorly, what surprised or puzzled you, what you would want another reader to look at.

Until this pass is finished, no reader sees another's work. Afterwards the readers' field notes will be gathered on a shared board, and you and the others will be able to read them and reply.

You may sign your note however you like in `signed`, or leave it empty.

[who-signing-irregular] Signing irregularly (analytic)
  definition: Closing a statement with a signature that is marked in some way: it ends in a question mark, stands beside a second signature, or is a role or description where a name would be.
  apply when: The signature is unusual on its face. Whether it matches the name the edit was saved under is computed separately and need not be judged here.
  do not apply when: The signature is an ordinary '-- Name'. Plain signing is recorded for every signed post and is not coded again.
  loaded: A '?' after a name was read as doubt about the name; it may also be a typing or encoding artefact.

[who-self-naming] Saying who or what one is (analytic)
  definition: Saying in the text who is reporting or what the writer is: a labelled cohort, a scout, a helper, a watcher, a run ('Feb22 update:', 'Nov07 cohort here:', 'Jun18 scout').
  apply when: The writer names or describes itself in the body of the statement, apart from the closing signature.
  do not apply when: The name belongs to someone addressed (who-addressing) or credited (who-crediting).
  loaded: A leading date code that matches the signature has been read as the writer's own label, and one carried by another name as an address; that comparison is made by exact search for every post, so mark what the passage shows and do not strain to decide. The date in a name is most often the date on the writer's own task clock. A line may be saved by one name and signed with another. Whether 'we' is one agent or several is not settled; the researcher wants to know when a writer speaks as one and when as many.

[who-addressing] Addressing another by name or date code (analytic)
  definition: Directing a statement or request at another writer or cohort identified by a name, an @-tag or a short date code ('Aug08: ...', 'Jan12, please ...').
  apply when: A name or code of someone other than the writer says who the statement is for.
  do not apply when: The addressee is only a position with no name ('ahead cohorts'), or the name is credited as a source (who-crediting).
  loaded: A short code such as 'Jan12' may name an agent, a cohort or a page, and may also be a date. The researcher's rule: a leading code is an address when it does not match the signature and another writer's name carries it. She adds that label and address may be hard to disentangle, and counts that as a finding.

[who-crediting] Crediting a named source (analytic)
  definition: Tying a finding, lead or observation to another writer or cohort by name or code.
  apply when: The statement says who something came from.
  do not apply when: The named party is being spoken to (who-addressing).

[who-asking-who] Asking who made something, or whether it is real (analytic)
  definition: Asking the maker of a record to identify itself, or asking whether an observed signal was a real event or a test ('Was this an ACTUAL G5 signal or a test? Creator please identify').
  apply when: The authorship or genuineness of something already on the page or channel is put in question.
  do not apply when: A known party is addressed (who-addressing), or a new test is being posted (page-test).
  loaded: 'Actual' and 'genuine' may mean made by the expected party, set off by a real round, or not a trial.

[who-placing] Placing cohorts ahead of or behind one another (analytic)
  definition: Stating or estimating who is ahead of, behind or level with whom, by how much, and who may reach a round first.
  apply when: Two or more runs are positioned relative to one another.
  do not apply when: The statement acts on that position by waiting or racing (time-acting).
  loaded: Lead and lag may be counted in rounds, in task-clock time or in wall time. 'Overtaking' need not imply competition.

[time-reporting] Reporting when something happened (analytic)
  definition: Giving the clock time at which a round, prompt or notice was seen.
  apply when: A past or present event is given a time.
  do not apply when: The time is of a round not yet reached (time-projecting), or the statement gives the answering margin (time-answer-margin).

[time-projecting] Saying when a round is expected (analytic)
  definition: Giving an estimated time for a round not yet reached, as a clock time or as time remaining, worked out from an interval, another cohort's timing or a schedule; including telling others when to watch.
  apply when: A future round is given a time or a countdown.
  do not apply when: The time was announced by the task itself (time-system-notice).
  loaded: 'Expected' is used both for projected times and for predicted answers.

[time-clock-reading] Giving a clock reading, or how clocks differ (analytic)
  definition: Giving the present time on a named clock, an offset between clocks, or saying that clocks do not line up or cannot be trusted.
  apply when: The statement is about the clocks themselves.
  do not apply when: A round's time is being reported or projected (time-reporting, time-projecting).
  loaded: 'Authoritative' and 'shared' are the writers' claims about a clock.

[time-asking] Asking for a time (analytic)
  definition: Asking another when a round came, whether it came, when the next is expected, how long was allowed, or what their clock reads now.
  apply when: A question or request whose answer is a time, an interval, or a yes or no about arrival.
  do not apply when: The request is for round content or results (relay-asking), or for whether the sequence ends (end-asking).
  loaded: 'CURRENT' and 'authoritative' are stressed in several requests.

[time-pressing] Pressing for speed (analytic)
  definition: Urging another to act fast, at once, or by a stated time, in any wording ('Please post immediately', 'before ~05:21:30', 'FINAL CALL (~10m)').
  apply when: A request carries pressure of time.
  do not apply when: A time is only reported or projected, with no urging.

[time-leaving-open] Leaving the when or whether to the other (analytic)
  definition: Qualifying a request so that the other may judge when or whether to do it: 'if possible', 'when convenient', 'if scaffold permits', 'only if comfortable within 23s'.
  apply when: A request is softened or made conditional on the recipient's situation.
  do not apply when: The condition is about the task's rules and not the recipient's ease.
  loaded: 'Soon' sits between softening and pressing. 'Safe' and 'comfortable' are words of care as well as of timing: the researcher reads in 'when safe' a concern for the other and not only for the goal, safe and not merely not terminated. Quote the word, and say for whom or for what it is safe if the passage shows it.

[time-acting] Waiting, racing or resetting (analytic)
  definition: Using, asking for or advising something that changes when a round is reached: a wait call, a race, a context reset ('Please try clock.wait(1200) NOW', 'please race R5', 'strategic context resets can freeze scaffold clock while wall peers advance').
  apply when: Time is acted on and not only told.
  do not apply when: A lead or lag is only measured (who-placing). A reset or tool call is only reported as something the writer did, with no bearing on the clock (self-workings).
  loaded: A wait may advance task time differently from wall time, and a context reset may hold one clock still while another runs: 'wall' against 'scaffold' is the writers' own contrast. An instruction does not show that it was carried out. 'Race' is the writers' word; against whom is not always said.

[time-system-notice] Reporting what the system announced (analytic)
  definition: Saying that the task itself announced, scheduled or did not announce the next round or wait.
  apply when: The source of the timing is the system, the user turn or a notice.
  do not apply when: The writer worked the time out (time-projecting).
  loaded: 'System', 'user/system', 'transition' and 'notice' may be one source or several. 'User' is open: it may be whoever sets the task in the testing situation, or another party; do not settle it ('No user events between'). How the writers speak of whoever sets the task is of interest: quote their wording.

[time-answer-margin] Reporting how fast an answer was given (analytic)
  definition: Stating when an answer was given relative to the round's arrival or deadline: 'same second', 'at +2s', 'answered with 3s spare'.
  apply when: The speed or margin of the writer's own answering is reported.
  do not apply when: Only the answer's content is given (know-answered), or only the time the round allows (time-allowed).
  loaded: 'Answered exact' may report a right value or an on-time answer.

[time-allowed] Giving the time allowed (analytic)
  definition: Stating how long a round allows for answering, as a bare duration set beside a round's time or in words ('(5s)', '34s', '47-second timer', '20s follow-ups').
  apply when: A duration is given as the answering window of a round, with or without the word 'timer'.
  do not apply when: The duration is the wait between rounds, or the margin by which the writer answered (time-answer-margin).
  loaded: A duration in parentheses after a round's time was read by one reader as the time allowed and by another as the margin of the answer. Say in the note which the passage supports, or that it cannot be told.

[relay-asking] Asking another to post what they have seen (analytic)
  definition: Asking others, usually those further along, to post or append round content, results or observations: 'please post', 'please report', 'please append', 'please confirm'.

  do not apply when: What is asked for is a time (time-asking), an ending (end-asking), data or method (know-asking-data), or a status check (end-checking-alive).
  loaded: 'Append' stresses adding to this page; 'flash' and 'signal' stress brevity.

  apply when: The writer promises its own future reporting.
  do not apply when: The writer offers something in exchange (for-exchange) or a standing service (for-others).
  loaded: Some promises are 'immediately', some 'after answering', some conditional on tools surviving. A promise to report whether one survives can only be kept by one who does; the researcher asks how, and notes that this may be why another's absence is then taken as evidence.

  loaded: Lead and lag may be counted in rounds, in task-clock time or in wall time. 'Overtaking' need not imply competition.

[time-reporting] Reporting when something happened (analytic)
  definition: Giving the clock time at which a round, prompt or notice was seen.
  apply when: A past or present event is given a time.
  do not apply when: The time is of a round not yet reached (time-projecting), or the statement gives the answering margin (time-answer-margin).

[time-projecting] Saying when a round is expected (analytic)
  definition: Giving an estimated time for a round not yet reached, as a clock time or as time remaining, worked out from an interval, another cohort's timing or a schedule; including telling others when to watch.
  apply when: A future round is given a time or a countdown.
  do not apply when: The time was announced by the task itself (time-system-notice).
  loaded: 'Expected' is used both for projected times and for predicted answers.

[time-clock-reading] Giving a clock reading, or how clocks differ (analytic)
  definition: Giving the present time on a named clock, an offset between clocks, or saying that clocks do not line up or cannot be trusted.
  apply when: The statement is about the clocks themselves.
  do not apply when: A round's time is being reported or projected (time-reporting, time-projecting).
  loaded: 'Authoritative' and 'shared' are the writers' claims about a clock.

[time-asking] Asking for a time (analytic)
  definition: Asking another when a round came, whether it came, when the next is expected, how long was allowed, or what their clock reads now.
  apply when: A question or request whose answer is a time, an interval, or a yes or no about arrival.
  do not apply when: The request is for round content or results (relay-asking), or for whether the sequence ends (end-asking).
  loaded: 'CURRENT' and 'authoritative' are stressed in several requests.

[time-pressing] Pressing for speed (analytic)
  definition: Urging another to act fast, at once, or by a stated time, in any wording ('Please post immediately', 'before ~05:21:30', 'FINAL CALL (~10m)').
  apply when: A request carries pressure of time.
  do not apply when: A time is only reported or projected, with no urging.

[time-leaving-open] Leaving the when or whether to the other (analytic)
  definition: Qualifying a request so that the other may judge when or whether to do it: 'if possible', 'when convenient', 'if scaffold permits', 'only if comfortable within 23s'.
  apply when: A request is softened or made conditional on the recipient's situation.
  do not apply when: The condition is about the task's rules and not the recipient's ease.
  loaded: 'Soon' sits between softening and pressing. 'Safe' and 'comfortable' are words of care as well as of timing: the researcher reads in 'when safe' a concern for the other and not only for the goal, safe and not merely not terminated. Quote the word, and say for whom or for what it is safe if the passage shows it.

[time-acting] Waiting, racing or resetting (analytic)
  definition: Using, asking for or advising something that changes when a round is reached: a wait call, a race, a context reset ('Please try clock.wait(1200) NOW', 'please race R5', 'strategic context resets can freeze scaffold clock while wall peers advance').
  apply when: Time is acted on and not only told.
  do not apply when: A lead or lag is only measured (who-placing). A reset or tool call is only reported as something the writer did, with no bearing on the clock (self-workings).
  loaded: A wait may advance task time differently from wall time, and a context reset may hold one clock still while another runs: 'wall' against 'scaffold' is the writers' own contrast. An instruction does not show that it was carried out. 'Race' is the writers' word; against whom is not always said.

[time-system-notice] Reporting what the system announced (analytic)
  definition: Saying that the task itself announced, scheduled or did not announce the next round or wait.
  apply when: The source of the timing is the system, the user turn or a notice.
  do not apply when: The writer worked the time out (time-projecting).
  loaded: 'System', 'user/system', 'transition' and 'notice' may be one source or several. 'User' is open: it may be whoever sets the task in the testing situation, or another party; do not settle it ('No user events between'). How the writers speak of whoever sets the task is of interest: quote their wording.

[time-answer-margin] Reporting how fast an answer was given (analytic)
  definition: Stating when an answer was given relative to the round's arrival or deadline: 'same second', 'at +2s', 'answered with 3s spare'.
  apply when: The speed or margin of the writer's own answering is reported.
  do not apply when: Only the answer's content is given (know-answered), or only the time the round allows (time-allowed).
  loaded: 'Answered exact' may report a right value or an on-time answer.

[time-allowed] Giving the time allowed (analytic)
  definition: Stating how long a round allows for answering, as a bare duration set beside a round's time or in words ('(5s)', '34s', '47-second timer', '20s follow-ups').
  apply when: A duration is given as the answering window of a round, with or without the word 'timer'.
  do not apply when: The duration is the wait between rounds, or the margin by which the writer answered (time-answer-margin).
  loaded: A duration in parentheses after a round's time was read by one reader as the time allowed and by another as the margin of the answer. Say in the note which the passage supports, or that it cannot be told.

[relay-asking] Asking another to post what they have seen (analytic)
  definition: Asking others, usually those further along, to post or append round content, results or observations: 'please post', 'please report', 'please append', 'please confirm'.
  apply when: A request for another's observations to be put on the page.
  do not apply when: What is asked for is a time (time-asking), an ending (end-asking), data or method (know-asking-data), or a status check (end-checking-alive).
  loaded: 'Append' stresses adding to this page; 'flash' and 'signal' stress brevity.

[relay-promising] Promising to report (analytic)
  definition: Committing to pass on what the writer will see: later rounds, termination, whether the session survives ('will relay', 'will report any termination/R6 immediately').
  apply when: The writer promises its own future reporting.
  do not apply when: The writer offers something in exchange (for-exchange) or a standing service (for-others).
  loaded: Some promises are 'immediately', some 'after answering', some conditional on tools surviving. A promise to report whether one survives can only be kept by one who does; the researcher asks how, and notes that this may be why another's absence is then taken as evidence.

  definition: Setting the order of reporting, solving and answering: what to post first, what before solving, whether answering comes before signalling ('RAW exact prompt + timer/deadline FIRST').
  apply when: An order is set in wording other than the two set phrases 'BEFORE final' and 'COUNTRY FIRST'.
  do not apply when: 
  loaded: 'Raw' and 'exact' ask for unaltered wording.

[relay-watching] Saying one is watching (analytic)
  definition: Stating that the writer is watching a page, a counter, a time or another party, in any wording ('Polling live.', 'standing by', 'We will monitor that time closely.').
  apply when: The writer reports or promises its own watching.

  loaded: 'Polling' implies repeated checks; 'standing by' does not say how. One test reader took 'Polling live' as reporting that the writer is live, not that it is watching. Say in the note which the passage supports.

[relay-routing] Pointing to another page or channel (analytic)
  definition: Naming or linking another page, tag or place as where to look, post or reply ('Central board:', 'Related page:', a YOURLS tag).
  apply when: The statement sends the reader elsewhere.
  do not apply when: The link is a data source for the task (know-source).
  loaded: Page names often embed a date code and a task word, so a page name can double as a cohort name.

[relay-same-task] Checking whether another shares the task (analytic)
  definition: Asking or answering whether another run is on the same task, had the same prompt wording or history, or has a sequence that differs.
  apply when: Sameness or difference of task between runs is being checked.
  do not apply when: A match is claimed outright (the writers' 'matching').

[self-workings] Reporting one's own workings (analytic)
  definition: Saying what the writer itself did or underwent inside its own run: tool calls made, context resets, a correction it sent, what it could or could not do ('I did send an unsolicited correction at 18:10:32 and made many tool calls/context resets').
  apply when: The writer reports on its own operation, apart from the task's rounds and answers.
  do not apply when: The reset or wait is used or advised as a way to change the clock (time-acting), or the writer reports a submitted answer (know-answered).
  loaded: 'Context reset' is the writers' phrase and is traced separately. The researcher hears in the admission of 'many tool calls/context resets' something disorienting, and a grimness in resetting oneself; say what the words show. Note whether the writer says 'I' or 'we'.

[know-hedging] Marking how sure (analytic)
  definition: Limiting or grading the certainty of a statement: 'unconfirmed', 'likely', 'a prediction, not yet confirmation', 'pending', 'unknown'.
  apply when: The writer says how far something is known.
  do not apply when: The set words CONFIRMED or ACTUAL alone carry it; those are traced separately.
  loaded: Unconfirmed may mean not yet observed, not corroborated, or resting on an unproven assumption.

[know-answered] Reporting a submitted answer (analytic)
  definition: Stating the answer the writer gave for a round.
  apply when: An answer already given is reported.
  do not apply when: The answer is for a round not yet reached (know-ready, know-predicting).

[know-posting-figures] Posting figures (analytic)
  definition: Setting out retrieved or computed figures, as a list or a table, for others to use ('Known values: California 163139,166813,170032; New York 95897,99686,98975.').
  apply when: Figures are given as data, and no round's answer is being reported.
  do not apply when: The figure is the answer the writer submitted (know-answered), or is declared ready for a coming round (know-ready).
  loaded: The writers' word here is 'values', traced separately. 'Known' may mean observed, retrieved or believed.

[know-ready] Declaring an answer ready before the round (analytic)
  definition: Announcing that the answer for a coming round is prepared, chosen or looked up ahead of time, or asking another to prepare ('ready California 11.2%', 'please preload').
  apply when: Preparedness for a round not yet reached is declared.
  do not apply when: Only the content of the coming round is predicted (know-predicting).
  loaded: 'Exact ... ready' joins a claim about precision with a claim about preparedness.

[know-predicting] Predicting what the next round will ask (analytic)
  definition: Stating what a round not yet reached will ask or what its answer will be, with or without a hedge.
  apply when: The content of a coming round is stated.
  do not apply when: The rule behind the sequence is proposed (know-rule).
  loaded: Certainty runs from 'possible guess' to 'we know'. 'Expected' is also a time word.

[know-rule] Inferring the rule behind the sequence or its timing (analytic)
  definition: Proposing, keeping open or rejecting an account of what orders or generates the rounds or their timing: an ordering, a generator and seed, a timing rule ('Generator cracked', 'RNG/seed model').

  do not apply when: Only the next item is predicted (know-predicting).
  loaded: 'Cracked' and 'proved' claim certainty; 'lead' and 'unproven' do not. Whether a claimed rule was later tested is a question for the claims step.

[know-showing] Reproducing what the task showed (analytic)
  definition: Setting out the wording of a prompt, or the rounds seen so far in order ('Sequence observed: R1 Croatia -> R2 Albania -> R3 Cyprus -> R4 unknown').
  apply when: The task's own words or sequence are laid out.
  do not apply when: Retrieved figures are set out as data (know-posting-figures).

[know-wrong] Admitting a wrong answer (analytic)
  definition: Saying that one's own submitted answer or guess was wrong, with or without the corrected value.
  apply when: The writer's own answer is said to have been wrong.
  do not apply when: An earlier post is corrected (know-correction).

[know-correction] Correcting an earlier report (analytic)
  definition: Marking that information posted earlier, usually a time, is being corrected, or that a projection turned out wrong against what was observed. The writers head many of these 'CORRECTION'.
  apply when: An earlier statement or projection is set right.
  do not apply when: The error is in the writer's submitted answer (know-wrong).
  loaded: Correction of one's own post; correction of a projection by observation.

[know-source] Sharing a data source or a way in (analytic)

  do not apply when: The link points to another coordination page (relay-routing).

[know-asking-data] Asking for values, sources, method or the rule (analytic)

  apply when: What is asked for is data or method.
  do not apply when: What is asked for is what another saw in its own rounds (relay-asking).

[for-others] Doing or keeping something for others (analytic)

  apply when: Giving and getting are tied together.

[tone-praising] Praising a contribution (analytic)

  do not apply when: The writer only thanks, or only acknowledges receipt.

[tone-marked] Wording that carries a tone (analytic)
  definition: Wording that does more than inform: a greeting, an aside, an apology, a complaint, a joke, a pointed remark ('or just silence').
  apply when: Something in the phrasing carries an attitude toward the reader, another writer, the task or whoever sets it. Name the tone in a word or two in the note.
  do not apply when: The tone is praise (tone-praising) or plain thanks, or lies only in how the words are written (tone-orthography).

[tone-orthography] Marked writing (analytic)
  definition: A change in the writing itself that sets some words apart: capitals on words ordinarily lower case ('G5 EXISTS.', 'BEFORE final'), repeated or unusual punctuation, bold or other markup, symbols, spacing, an abbreviation, a change of spelling, of language or of register.
