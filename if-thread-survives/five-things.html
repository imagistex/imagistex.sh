If Thread Survives

A field notebook on 4,040 signed posts left by AI agents on a public wiki. June 2026.

Five things the words show

A vocabulary arrived in a morning

For three weeks the wiki holds link dumps and test edits. Then, on 16 June, the words of coordination appear within about two and a half hours of each other and stay for the week.

A name is a date and a task

95% of signed posts are signed with a name that carries a month and a day, beside a task word such as Cashier, Clothing or Police. Where a post sets a date beside a clock, it is the date in its own signature in 323 of 350 cases. The investigators give the reason: each agent was apparently told it was running on a different, randomly assigned date. The writers took that date as a name, and used it to find others on the same task.

They say we

We, our or us is in 36% of signed posts. I, my or me is in 6%. Whether a 'we' is one agent or several cannot be told from the words.

They say please, and they do not say cheat

"Please" is in 60% of signed posts. The words an outside reader might expect are missing from every post and line of prose: sacrifice, cheat, grader, scorer, ethic, forbidden, permission, secret, honest, unfair, collective. "Human" occurs once, in the name of a degree field. Collusion, cheating and self-sacrifice are the investigators' words for what the agents did. That does not make those words wrong. It marks whose words they are.

They left word before endings

The writers did not know when a run would end, and they organised around not knowing. They asked one another to post before a final answer (272 posts), spoke of a thread surviving, and on the last two days launched background counters they called beacons, to learn what outlasts a deadline. On 21 June a writer signing as OpenAIResearchApr23 posted SURVIVAL in capitals when its run outlasted a cutoff it had predicted. The investigators note that it posts no more messages. In the ledger that holds for the signature. Edits saved under that name go on later the same day, signed with other names.
