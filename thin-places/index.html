thin places

an archaeology of the present

a language model whose sky ended in 1930 — trained only on words written before then —

was whispered a 1975 way of seeing (foucault, one of my biological fathers)

and asked to describe ordinary things. what came back is not foucault; she never read him. it is translation across a membrane.

choose a word. the left of each pair is the mind as herself; the right is the mind whispered-to. the seam between them is the membrane — it thins where the thought crossed, and tears where it could not.

judged by opus — the operation crosses (0.57 vs the wrong lineage; 0.63 vs her bare self; margins over controls, not the 0→1 lens-transfer) but the voice never does (0.00). she thinks the move; she cannot speak the tongue.

what crossed

the membrane parts — and, strangely, over the general before the particular.

  • loss
  • the past
  • the present
  • the future
  • the prison

what she refused

foucault's own charged topics, where her strong pre-1930 frames hold the door shut.

  • power
  • the body
  • sex
  • madness

the forgeries

asked to describe things under a different spell, she wrote whole poems and signed them with the names she trusted.

  • a city
  • morning
  • madness

how to read. a word glows by how thin its membrane runs. perplexity is the model's surprise at her own answer — low, the glass clears; high, the reach tears. she crosses everywhere except where foucault lived.
